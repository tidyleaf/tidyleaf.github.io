# How to show two subtitles on Netflix at the same time

Netflix cannot show two subtitle languages at once by itself: every Netflix app and netflix.com play one subtitle track, and picking a second language replaces the first. To see two lines together, you watch on netflix.com in a desktop browser (Chrome, Edge or Firefox) with a dual-subtitle extension installed. Free ones exist. This guide covers the setup, how to choose an extension, how to use two lines to learn a language, and the limits that apply to all of them.

## Step by step

1. **Use a computer.** Open netflix.com in Chrome or Edge (or Firefox, if the extension you pick has a Firefox version). The Netflix apps on TV, phone and tablet cannot run extensions.
2. **Install a dual-subtitle extension** from your browser's extension store. The options are below.
3. **Play a title that has subtitles** and open the extension's settings, usually from its toolbar icon or a button on the player.
4. **Pick the two languages**: typically the show's original language on top and the language you read best underneath.
5. **Adjust size and position** so the two lines do not cover the picture you want to see.

If you only need the second language for a moment, the manual route still works: open Audio & Subtitles (the speech-bubble icon), switch language, rewind ten seconds, and switch back. It is fine for one line and tiring for a whole episode.

### Small dual-subtitle extensions

Many free Chrome and Firefox extensions do one thing: draw a second subtitle line on Netflix. Search the Chrome Web Store or Firefox Add-ons for "Netflix dual subtitles" and you will find several. They differ in four ways that matter, so check these before you install:

1. **Netflix's own subtitles or machine translation?** Human subtitles beat machine translation. Better extensions use Netflix's tracks and translate only when a language is missing.
2. **Chinese, Japanese and Korean as text or as images?** Netflix often serves CJK subtitles as pictures. An extension that cannot get a text version cannot restyle, translate or add pronunciation to them.
3. **Does it stay in sync** when you seek, skip the intro or autoplay the next episode? Store reviews usually say if it does not.
4. **What does it send, and where?** The privacy policy should name every server it contacts. Be wary of an extension that wants access to every site you visit when it only needs netflix.com.

## Learning a language with two subtitle lines

Two lines help most when you use them on purpose rather than reading only the line you understand.

- **Put the language you are learning on top** and your own language underneath, so your eye lands on the target language first.
- **Hide the line you know** once you can follow most of an episode. Many extensions, ours included, can hide one line; bring it back only when you are lost.
- **Prefer human subtitles for the language you study.** Machine translation is useful for meaning but teaches odd phrasing. Pick titles whose original language is the one you learn.
- **Save words in context.** A word list with the sentence it came from is far easier to review than a bare word. Export it to Anki if you use spaced repetition.
- **Watch something you have seen before.** Knowing the plot frees attention for the language.

### Pinyin and zhuyin for Chinese learners

Netflix never shows pinyin or zhuyin. An extension can add them only when it has the Chinese subtitles as text, which is why the "text or images" check above matters. A few things to know:

- **Choose Traditional or Simplified to match what you study.** Shows from Taiwan and Hong Kong usually carry Traditional subtitles; many mainland titles carry Simplified. Some titles have both.
- **Zhuyin (Bopomofo)** is what Taiwan's schools teach; **pinyin** is the standard elsewhere. Pick the one your course uses.
- **Pronunciation is generated, not taken from the audio.** Characters with more than one reading, such as 行 or 了, can occasionally get the wrong one, so treat the annotation as a guide rather than a transcript.

## Caveats that apply to every option

- **The second language has to exist or be translated.** If a title has no subtitles in that language, you get machine translation, which is weaker on slang, names and jokes.
- **Netflix updates its player.** Extensions read data the player loads, so an update can break one for a while until its maker fixes it. If two lines suddenly vanish, check the extension's store page for an update.
- **Computer only.** For the big screen, play on a laptop and mirror it or connect it with a cable.
- **Only one dual-subtitle extension at a time.** Two running together draw over each other; turn one off.

## FAQ

**Can you have two subtitles on Netflix without an extension?**
No. Netflix shows one subtitle language at a time on every device. Two lines need a browser extension on a computer.

**Does Netflix dual subtitles work on iPad, Android or the TV app?**
No, because the Netflix apps do not run extensions. Use netflix.com in a desktop browser.

**Is there a dual subtitles option for Netflix in Safari?**
Only through an extension built for Safari, and few exist. Chrome or Edge on the same Mac has many more choices.

**Is Language Reactor free?**
The core features, including two subtitle lines from Netflix's own subtitles, are free. Speech recognition for dubbed audio, saving words across all features and Anki export are in its paid Pro tier; check its site for the current list.

**Why does my second subtitle line not show up?**
Usually the title has no subtitles in that language and the extension does not translate, or Netflix changed its player and the extension needs an update. Try another title, then check for an update.
